Conformal Triage Layer for Chest X-ray Screening

A post-hoc wrapper that turns any chest X-ray abnormality classifier into a screening triage layer with a finite-sample safety guarantee. You choose the miss rate you can live with; the layer removes as much of the worklist as that budget allows. Every number below is computed live in your browser from real inference on real NIH studies.

3 · Where the threshold falls

Normal studies Abnormal studies Auto-cleared region

Note the x-axis: these real scores occupy roughly 0.52–0.73, not 0–1. They are a max over 14 sigmoid outputs and are badly miscalibrated — a study scoring “0.63” is nowhere near 63% likely to be abnormal. The guarantee holds anyway, exactly. Conformal calibration uses only the ranking of scores, never their face value, which is precisely why it can wrap a model whose confidence numbers you do not trust — the common case in deployed imaging AI.

7 · What this does and does not claim

  • Guaranteed: P(auto-cleared | abnormal) ≤ α. A study that is truly abnormal has at most an α chance of being dropped from the worklist. This is a finite-sample, distribution-free bound — no asymptotics, no assumption that the model is calibrated.
  • Not guaranteed: P(abnormal | auto-cleared), the share of the cleared pile that is abnormal. That quantity moves with disease prevalence and gets no promise here. Conflating the two is the standard error in this space, so the code names them separately.
  • Assumption: calibration and incoming studies are exchangeable. Scanner changes, case-mix shifts, or a model update all break this and require recalibration. The layer is honest but not self-monitoring.
  • Marginal, not training-conditional. The bound holds in expectation over calibration draws — but a deployment draws one calibration set and keeps it. The ± spread above is the cost: at α=0.05 a realized deployment can land near 0.063. A PAC-style bound would instead promise “miss rate ≤ α with 95% confidence” for a few points of worklist cut. Not claimed here; it is the most valuable next change.
  • Scope: binary normal/abnormal screening triage, retrospective, not PACS-integrated. The guarantee is per-study and marginal, not per-subgroup. All splits here are patient-disjoint, but a per-patient guarantee is a different and stronger claim.
  • Not novel math. Split conformal prediction and selective classification are established. The contribution is packaging: a model-agnostic layer with one dial a non-ML radiologist can actually set and defend.
